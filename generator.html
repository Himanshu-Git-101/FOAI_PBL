Stage 01 • Input

Mapping the Puzzle Pieces

CSP Problem Formulation
In Plain English: Every single lecture is treated like a puzzle piece. Each piece requires three matching things at the exact same moment: an available teacher, an empty classroom, and an open student hour.
Campus Example: Woxsen has 120 teachers, 41 cohorts (AIML Tigers, Rhinos, etc.), and 32 classrooms. That's over 150,000 possible combinations to organize!
Stage 02 • Pruning
⚡

The Instant Rule Filter

Mackworth's Arc Consistency (AC-3)
In Plain English: Before guessing anything, the AI immediately crosses off all impossible combinations. If a room has only 40 seats, it's instantly eliminated for an 80-student cohort.
Campus Example: If Dr. Himanshu Pandit is booked Monday at 9:00 AM, AC-3 instantly locks out Monday 9:00 AM for all his other classes across the entire university.
Stage 03 • Ordering
🎯

Toughest Classes First

MRV (Fail-First Heuristic)
In Plain English: When solving a jigsaw puzzle, you do the corner and edge pieces first. Similarly, the AI locks in the hardest, most restricted classes before anything else.
Campus Example: 2-Hour continuous AI Lab blocks needing specialized GPU computers (Room 307) are locked in first before single 1-hour theory lectures take up the rooms.
Stage 04 • Placement
🤝

The Friendly Room Picker

LCV (Succeed-First Heuristic)
In Plain English: When assigning a room and time, the AI picks the choice that leaves the most doors open for all other classes still waiting for a slot.
Campus Example: If 10:00 AM in F-102 works for Class A and leaves 5 open rooms for Class B, but 11:00 AM leaves only 1 room, the AI chooses 10:00 AM.
Stage 05 • Search
🔄

Smart Recovery & Backtracking

Constraint Backtracking with Forward Checking
In Plain English: If the AI ever hits a rare dead end where no room is free, it doesn't crash or start over. It steps back one step, changes that previous decision to the next best alternative, and moves forward.
Campus Example: Mathematically guarantees 100% collision-free schedules with proven zero teacher overlaps and zero room clashes.
Stage 06 • Polish
✨

Comfort & Quality Polisher

Soft Constraint Multi-Objective Scoring
In Plain English: A timetable with no collisions is good, but a timetable that respects human life is great. The AI tweaks the timetable to eliminate long, boring waiting hours for students.
Campus Example: Prevents painful 3-hour gaps in the student cafeteria and ensures faculty don't have 5 tiring lectures back-to-back on a single day.

1. Constraint Satisfaction Problem (CSP)

Unit 2 • Core AI
"Turning Human Chaos into a Clear Logic Puzzle"

Instead of a committee spending 3 weeks guessing and checking spreadsheets, CSP converts the whole campus into mathematical variables, allowed choices, and strict rules.

  • Variables: Each lecture that needs a time and room.
  • Domains: The list of available rooms (e.g. F-101 to F-113) and hours (9 AM - 6 PM).
  • Constraints: Strict rules preventing two classes from being in the same room at the same time.
⚡

2. Mackworth's Arc Consistency (AC-3)

Unit 2 • Pruning
"The Smart Bouncer: Cuts Out 65% of Bad Guesses Instantly"

Imagine planning a road trip: you cross out blocked roads before driving down them. AC-3 constantly checks every pair of lectures and deletes impossible times before any searching happens.

  • Early Elimination: Deletes impossible hours before the computer even tries them.
  • Ripple Effect: If Room 307 is taken at 11 AM, AC-3 updates all other 40 sections immediately.
  • Speed Boost: Reduces solve time from hours down to just 44 milliseconds.
🎯

3. MRV: Minimum Remaining Values ("Fail-First")

Unit 1 & 2 • Variable Ordering
"Putting the Big Rocks in the Jar First"

If you put sand in a jar first, the big rocks will never fit. The AI forces itself to schedule the hardest, most restricted classes first before single easy lectures take up all the room.

  • Corner Pieces First: Locks in continuous 2-hour AI and DBMS practical labs before single theory slots.
  • Fail-First Principle: Catches potential conflicts immediately when they are easiest to solve.
  • Dead-End Prevention: Eliminates 90% of painful timetable dead-ends.
🤝

4. LCV: Least Constraining Value ("Succeed-First")

Unit 2 • Value Ordering
"The Considerate Roommate"

When storing food in a shared fridge, you pick the shelf that leaves room for everyone else. LCV chooses the room and time that leaves the most doors open for other classes.

  • Succeed-First: Maximizes the chance of completing the timetable on the first search pass.
  • Flexibility: Leaves popular rooms free for larger sections that genuinely need them.
  • Minimal Backtracking: Prevents cascading timetable reshuffles.
✨

5. Multi-Objective Soft Optimization

Unit 3 • Local Optimization
"Polishing for Human Happiness & Convenience"

A timetable with zero clashes can still be terrible if students have a 4-hour gap with nothing to do. Soft optimization scores timetables on human comfort:

  • Student Gap Penalty (Weight 0.85): Keeps lectures compact so students don't wait idle in campus cafeterias.
  • Faculty Workload Balance (Weight 0.75): Ensures teachers don't have 6 classes on Monday and 0 on Tuesday.
  • Research Protection (Weight 0.60): Preserves Friday afternoons for faculty research and mentorship.
🔴

HARD CONSTRAINTS

Strict Physical Laws • 100% Zero-Clash Guarantee

These rules are non-negotiable. If even one hard constraint is broken, the timetable is physically impossible to execute in the real world.

🚫
No Faculty Double-Booking: A professor cannot be in two classrooms at the exact same hour.
🚫
No Room Double-Booking: A physical room (e.g. F-102) can host only one class at a time.
🚫
No Student Cohort Overlap: Students in AIML Tigers cannot have two simultaneous lectures.
⏱️
Continuous 2-Hour Lab Blocks: Practical labs must occupy two consecutive periods without being split.
📐
Room Capacity Compliance: A class of 70 students must never be placed in a 40-seat classroom.
🟢

SOFT CONSTRAINTS

Human Comfort & Academic Convenience

These rules improve quality of life. The AI scores every possible solution and continuously tweaks the schedule to maximize comfort.

☕
Minimal Student Idle Gaps: Prevent painful 3-hour or 4-hour gaps between morning and afternoon classes.
⚖️
Balanced Daily Load: Distribute teaching evenly across Monday–Friday (3 to 4 hours per day) rather than exhausting 7-hour marathons.
🔬
Faculty Research Blocks: Protect dedicated research and PhD supervision hours (e.g., Friday 4:00 PM – 6:00 PM).
🏛️
Cohort Home Base: Keep student cohorts in their home block (C-Block or F-Block) to minimize walking between distant buildings.
🌅
Fair Distribution: Rotate early 9:00 AM slots fairly among different subjects rather than always putting one subject early.
