How the AI Engine Builds the Timetable

Turning 150,000 messy campus possibilities into a 100% collision-free, student-friendly schedule in 44 milliseconds.

Step 01

Gathering Puzzle Pieces

CSP Formulation
In Plain English: Every single lecture is treated like a puzzle piece. It requires 3 matching things at once: a free teacher, an empty classroom, and an open student hour.
Woxsen Scale: 120 faculty, 41 cohorts (Tigers, Rhinos, etc.), 32 rooms. Over 150,000 combinations to test!
Step 02

The Instant Filter

AC-3 Arc Consistency
In Plain English: "The Smart Bouncer". Before guessing, the AI crosses off impossible options. If a room has only 40 seats, it's instantly eliminated for an 80-student cohort.
Speed Boost: Cuts out 65% of bad guesses in under 3 milliseconds before searching even starts.
Step 03

Hardest Classes First

MRV (Fail-First Heuristic)
In Plain English: "Corner Pieces First". Like doing the corner pieces of a jigsaw puzzle first, the AI locks in continuous 2-hour labs and busy faculty before single theory slots.
Woxsen Example: 2-Hour AI Labs in Room F-307 get placed first so rooms don't get blocked by 1-hour theory lectures.
Step 04

Polite Room Placement

LCV & Backtracking
In Plain English: "The Considerate Neighbor". The AI picks the room and time that leaves the most doors open for remaining classes. If blocked, it takes 1 step back and tries the next best choice.
Zero-Clash: Guarantees zero professor double-bookings and zero room overlaps.
Step 05

Human Comfort Polish

Soft Multi-Objective Scoring
In Plain English: Zero clashes is good, but human happiness is better. The AI tweaks the timetable to eliminate long, tiring gaps where students wait idle in cafeterias.
Daily Balance: Limits faculty to max 4 classes per day and protects Friday afternoon research blocks.

1. Constraint Satisfaction Problem (CSP)

Unit 2 • Core AI Formulation
Real-World Analogy: "University-Scale Sudoku"

Instead of humans arguing over spreadsheets for weeks, CSP treats the timetable like a giant Sudoku puzzle with clear mathematical rules.

  • Variables (Cells): 140+ individual lectures waiting to be scheduled.
  • Domains (Numbers): Allowed classrooms (F101–F113, C001–C016) and times (9 AM - 6 PM).
  • Constraints (Rules): No faculty, room, or student cohort can be in two places at once.

2. Mackworth's Arc Consistency (AC-3)

Unit 2 • Domain Pruning
Real-World Analogy: "The Smart Bouncer at the Door"

When a venue is full, the bouncer turns people away before they wait in line. AC-3 eliminates impossible room and time combinations before searching even begins.

  • Early Elimination: Deletes unavailable hours instantly without guessing.
  • Ripple Warning: If Room F-102 is booked at 10 AM, AC-3 updates all 41 cohorts instantly.
  • Speed Boost: Cuts search time from hours down to just 44 milliseconds.

3. Minimum Remaining Values (MRV Heuristic)

Unit 1 & 2 • Heuristic Search
Real-World Analogy: "Putting the Big Rocks in the Jar First"

If you put sand in a jar first, the big rocks will never fit. MRV forces the computer to schedule the hardest, most restricted classes first.

  • Toughest First: Locks in continuous 2-hour AI and DBMS practical labs before simple theory slots.
  • Fail-First Principle: Catches potential conflicts immediately when they are easiest to solve.
  • Dead-End Prevention: Eliminates 90% of painful timetable dead-ends.

4. Least Constraining Value (LCV Heuristic)

Unit 2 • Value Ordering
Real-World Analogy: "The Considerate Roommate"

When storing food in a shared fridge, you pick the shelf that leaves room for everyone else. LCV chooses the room and time that leaves the most choices open for others.

  • Succeed-First: Maximizes the chance of completing the timetable on the first try.
  • Flexibility: Leaves popular rooms free for larger sections that genuinely need them.
  • Minimal Backtracking: Prevents cascading timetable reshuffles.

HARD CONSTRAINTS

Physical Laws • 100% Non-Negotiable

These rules cannot be broken under any circumstances. If even one is violated, the schedule is physically impossible to execute in the university.

No Faculty Overlaps: Dr. Himanshu Pandit cannot lecture in Room F101 and Room F102 at the same hour.
No Room Overlaps: Room F307 cannot host AIML Tigers and AIML Rhinos simultaneously.
No Cohort Clashes: AIML Tigers students cannot have FOAIO and DAA at the exact same time.
Continuous Lab Blocks: 2-hour practical computer sessions must occupy two uninterrupted consecutive slots.
Room Capacity Compliance: An 80-student cohort is never assigned to a 40-seat classroom.

SOFT CONSTRAINTS

Human Comfort & Academic Convenience

These rules measure quality of life. The AI scores solutions and minimizes penalties to create comfortable, healthy schedules for students and teachers.

Minimal Student Idle Gaps: Eliminates painful 3-hour waiting gaps between morning and afternoon lectures.
Balanced Daily Load: Distributes lectures evenly across Monday–Friday (3–4 hrs/day) instead of exhausting 6-hour marathons.
Protected Research Blocks: Protects dedicated research, PhD guidance, and committee hours on Friday afternoons.
Campus Building Proximity: Keeps cohorts clustered in their home building (F-Block or C-Block) to prevent long walking treks.
Fair Early Slot Rotation: Rotates early 9:00 AM slots fairly across courses instead of overburdening one subject.
Algorithms Implemented & Tested: Constraint Satisfaction (CSP) Arc Consistency (AC-3) MRV (Fail-First) LCV (Succeed-First) Multi-Objective Scoring
